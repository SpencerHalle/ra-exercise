# Research Assistant Exercise

Hey! Thanks for applying, and for being willing to try this short exercise.
We wanted to keep it small so it doesn't eat your week, and we want to see
how you approach data you haven't seen before.

You don't need a perfect analysis, and you don't need to use the methods we
would have used. We just want to see how you think. Do it whenever works for
you. We expect it to take **about an hour**. Please don't spend more than
two. Unfinished is fine. We all have things to do.

## Who can apply

If you're an undergrad, you need to be **graduating in Fall 2026 or Spring
2027**. This position isn't open to current juniors.

## ⚠️ Please do this without AI

This is the one rule we really care about, so here it is up front:

**No AI tools of any kind.** That means no ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot,
or anything like them. That covers writing your code, analyzing the data,
making your slides, and writing your answers.

- Turn off AI autocomplete in your editor (GitHub Copilot, Cursor, the
  built-in AI in VS Code or MATLAB, etc.).
- Googling, Stack Overflow, and official documentation are all fine. Just
  skip the AI-generated summaries at the top of search results.
- Asking a person how a plotting function works is fine. Asking a person
  (or a chatbot) to do the problem for you is not.

Why? An AI could solve this whole thing in a few seconds, and then we'd
learn nothing about *you*. This is a pretty simple problem on purpose. We'd
much rather see your own rough, honest attempt than a polished one that
isn't yours. A messy plot with your own reasoning will beat a perfect plot
you can't explain. It's more fun that way, too. We're not against AI in
general (we use it in our research), but for this exercise it works better
without it.

## Other rules

- **Tools:** use a programming language: Python, MATLAB, R, Julia, C++,
  whatever you're comfortable with. **No spreadsheet software** (Excel,
  Google Sheets). We want to see how you work in code.
- **Work alone,** and please don't post your solution publicly, fork this
  repo, or open a pull request. Just download it, and send your work to
  us directly (see below).

## Background

A lab is making a polymer and wants to get as much product out of each
reaction as possible. They can control two things:

- **Reaction temperature**, in °C (they can go anywhere from 60 to 120)
- **Monomer-to-initiator ratio** (they can go anywhere from 50 to 400)

For each experiment they measure the **yield**, which is the percent of the
starting material that ended up as product (0–100%). Each experiment takes
about a full day, so they can't just try everything.

The file `experiments.csv` has the 18 experiments run so far. To get it,
click the green **Code** button at the top of this page and choose
**Download ZIP**.

## Questions

1. **Look at the data.** Make one or two plots that you think are useful for
   understanding it. Briefly say why you chose them.

2. **What do you notice?** Describe any patterns or trends you see. Is there
   anything you would or wouldn't expect?

3. **Plan the next experiments.** The lab can only afford **three** more
   experiments. Which temperature and ratio would you pick for each one, and
   why? There's no single right answer. Just explain your thinking.

4. **Explain it simply.** In 3 sentences or fewer, write what you'd tell the
   lab manager (who is busy and not a data person) about what the data shows
   so far and what you recommend.

## What to make: a few slides

Put your answers in a **short PowerPoint, about 3 to 5 slides.** Keep it
simple. Pretend you're quickly explaining your work to the lab at a group
meeting. Something like this works well:

1. Your plot(s) and why you chose them (Question 1)
2. What you noticed (Question 2)
3. Your three proposed experiments and why (Question 3)
4. Your 3-sentence summary for the lab manager (Question 4)

Bullet points are fine. Nothing needs to be fancy, and you don't need
more than a sentence or two per question, except Question 3, which can be a
little longer. Please add one line somewhere on the first slide saying:
*"I completed this exercise without using AI tools."*
